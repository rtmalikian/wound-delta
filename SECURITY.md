# Security and honest limits

WoundDelta handles photos of people's wounds. That is health data, and in
many homes it is also intimate data. This document states plainly what the
software does, what it does not do, and what a real deployment must add.

## What this software is

Measurement and trending software. It aligns two photos of the same wound,
measures how the segmented area and redness changed, checks four stated
pattern rules, and phrases the result. **It is not a medical device. It does
not diagnose any condition. It does not assess infection. It does not
recommend treatment, dressings, medication, or whether to seek care.** A
red-flag flag means exactly this: a measured pattern crossed a stated
threshold, and the output says to raise it with the care team.

## The regulatory boundary, stated plainly

Software that measures wound size and trends it for clinician review is
positioned here as information and decision support. Software that claims
to diagnose, stage, or direct treatment of wounds can fall under
medical-device regulation (in the US, FDA device rules; elsewhere, the
local equivalent). This project deliberately stays on the measurement side
of that line: no condition names in outputs, no severity grades, no
treatment language. If you extend it, know that adding diagnostic claims
can change its regulatory status, and get real regulatory advice first.
Nothing in this repository is legal or regulatory advice.

## Accuracy limits

- The eval numbers in the README (area-change MAE 0.07, red-flag recall
  1.0) are measured on **synthetic fixtures**: drawn ellipses on a
  skin-tone field, generated by code in this repo. They prove the pipeline
  computes what it claims to compute. They do not prove accuracy on real
  wounds, real skin tones, tattoos, dressings, hair, poor light, or shaky
  phone photos. Real-photo accuracy is unknown until someone runs the
  labelled validation set in roadmap issue #1.
- Segmentation is a two-channel colour heuristic (strong redness, or much
  darker than skin), not a trained model. It will fail on wounds whose
  colours sit outside those channels, and the failure mode is a wrong area
  number presented with the same confidence as a right one. The ghost
  overlay mitigates angle and distance error; it cannot fix colour failure.
- Pattern thresholds (expansion +25%, spread 15%, darkening 18 points,
  stall 14 days) are stated constants chosen for the fixtures, not clinical
  guidelines. A clinician deployment should set its own.

## Photo and PHI handling

- **Self-hosting is the privacy story.** Run the `local` backend (Ollama,
  llama.cpp, vLLM with a vision model) and photos never leave the home or
  clinic hardware. The default `stub` backend also sends nothing anywhere:
  summaries are templated from the measurements.
- **The `upstream` backend is a trade-off, not a default.** Point the
  gateway at a third-party provider and image crops (frugal tier) or full
  frames (full tier) leave the building. That decision belongs to the
  deploying organisation, with the right agreements in place.
- **Labels pass the redaction gate** (`app/safety.py`): emails, phone
  numbers, SSNs, dates of birth, MRNs, member IDs, street addresses, and
  labelled patient names are stripped from case labels before storage.
  Logs record redaction kinds and counts, never values. Photos themselves
  are not redacted; treat stored captures as PHI.
- **Storage in v0.1.0 is in-memory.** Restarting the app drops cases and
  photos. That is a pilot limitation, and in one sense a safety property.
  A real deployment needs encrypted storage, access control, retention
  rules, and audit logs; none of those ship here and none should be
  improvised.

## The human rule

A clinician reviews the trend. The patient-facing summary says so, the
flags say "raise this with your care team", and no setting in this codebase
turns a flag into an action taken without a human. Do not wire comparisons
to automatic messages to patients without clinical governance.
